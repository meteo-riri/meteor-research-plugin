# Academic English Writing Guide — Reviewer Responses

## Tone

- Professional, respectful, and concise
- Acknowledge the reviewer's effort before disagreeing
- Avoid defensive language
- Be specific: cite line numbers, figures, tables when they exist

## Common Sentence Patterns

**Acknowledging a valid point:**
- "We thank Reviewer N for this insightful comment."
- "We appreciate the reviewer's careful reading."
- "This is a valid concern that we have addressed as follows."

**Explaining a revision:**
- "We have revised {X} to better reflect {Y}. [LINE NUMBERS TO BE ADDED]"
- "The text now reads: '...'"
- "We have added a sentence clarifying that..."

**Addressing a methodological concern:**
- "We used {method} because {scientific reason}."
- "This approach is consistent with {prior work} [PROPOSED CITATION]."
- "We acknowledge this limitation and have added a statement to the Discussion."

**Politely disagreeing:**
- "We respectfully disagree with this interpretation."
- "While we understand the reviewer's perspective, we believe..."
- "We note that {alternative explanation}, which supports our interpretation."

**When data is not available:**
- "Unfortunately, {X} was not feasible in the current study due to {reason}."
- "We agree this would strengthen the manuscript; we plan to address this in future work."
- "[AUTHOR INPUT REQUIRED: confirm whether this experiment can be added]"

## Style Rules

- Use active voice where possible
- Keep paragraphs to 3–5 sentences
- Spell out acronyms at first use in the response letter
- Do not use contractions
- British or American English — be consistent throughout the letter
- Numbers: spell out one through nine; use numerals for 10 and above
